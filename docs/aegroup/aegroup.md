# Algorithm Engineering Group

Led by **[Professor Yi Zhou](../index_en.md)** in the [Algorithms and Logic Lab](https://tcsuestc.com/) at UESTC, our group studies algorithms that combine sound theory with strong practical performance.

Our work spans algorithm design, analysis, implementation, optimization, profiling, and experimental evaluation. We develop exact algorithms, heuristics, integer programming formulations, and parallel methods for combinatorial optimization.

[Joining guidance](#suggestions-for-future-students){ .md-button .md-button--primary }
[Email Yi Zhou](mailto:zhou.yi@uestc.edu.cn){ .md-button }

## What we work on

- **Graph optimization**: relaxed cliques, Steiner trees, and related combinatorial problems. [Explore relaxed-clique algorithms](../research/relaxcliquesolver/relaxedclique.md).
- **Industrial optimization**: two- and three-dimensional cutting-stock problems and satellite scheduling.
- **Optimization in AI**: submodular optimization, decision trees, and applications of large language models to optimization algorithms. [Explore submodular optimization](../research/submodular/submax.md).

We discuss research in weekly group meetings and take part in the lab's [seminars](https://tcsuestc.com/seminars/). Students have received honors including two UESTC Outstanding Student awards for undergraduates, Huawei's Top Minds recognition, university outstanding thesis awards, and national scholarships.

## Suggestions for future students

### Undergraduates

UESTC undergraduates are welcome to contact us throughout the year by email or by visiting my office. Research training offers preparation for further study at the master's or PhD level.

### Master's applicants

Consider whether you enjoy combinatorial problem solving, algorithm design and analysis, and implementing and optimizing programs. Much of our performance-oriented work uses C/C++. Students should be able to participate in weekly group activities and devote one month each semester to research training.

### PhD applicants

Introduce the research problems you would like to explore and how your experience relates to our work. Please contact me to discuss research fit and opportunities.

### A useful first email

The following is a suggested checklist, rather than a formal application requirement:

1. Your current university, degree level, and intended stage of study.
2. The research direction or problem that interests you.
3. Relevant coursework, programming projects, or research experience, with links if available.
4. Your thoughts or questions about one of our papers or software projects.

Write to **[zhou.yi@uestc.edu.cn](mailto:zhou.yi@uestc.edu.cn)**. During peak application periods, I may be unable to reply to every inquiry.
